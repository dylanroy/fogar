Straight talk

Offline reads print. Handwriting needs a model.

The rest of Fogar is a new tab that answers from a model running in your browser. See what leaves in each mode.

  • Tesseract reads printed text, not handwriting. When it finds nothing, the widget says so and points you at a vision model.
  • English only on the device, for now. Other languages are on the roadmap, not in the build.
  • A cloud vision model means the photo goes to that provider, with your key, straight from your browser. There is no Fogar server in between.
  • Plain text only: no formatting, no search across pages, no sync. Everything lives in this browser and rides along in the backup under Settings.
  • The first photo takes a moment while the reader starts. It lets go of its memory after two quiet minutes.
Questions

Fair questions, short answers.

Can it turn handwriting into text offline?

Not with the built-in reader, which is made for printed text. It can if you run a vision model in Ollama on the same computer and pick it as the reader: the photo goes to localhost and nowhere else. How well it reads depends on the model.

Does my photo leave my computer?

Only if you pick a cloud model as the reader. On this device, and with Ollama on this computer, nothing leaves. With a cloud model, the scaled-down photo goes to that provider’s endpoint with your key.

Is the photo saved anywhere?

No. Fogar scales it down, reads it, and adds the text to the page. The photo itself is not stored, in the notebook or in the backup.

What happens when I drag a sticky note onto another card?

Onto another Post-its board, the note moves there. Onto any other card, such as your todos or the agenda, the note becomes its own Post-its widget next to that card. Drop it back on its own board and it just moves.

Where are my notes kept?

In this browser’s extension storage. Notebook pages, sticky notes, and the scratchpad all ride along in the backup file under Settings, and the Notebook also downloads as Markdown. None of it syncs between computers.
