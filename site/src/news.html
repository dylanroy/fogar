How it works

Add sources. Open tabs. Read the top.

Add the Feed widget

Add widget, then Feed. Pick “Feed or site address” or one of the four searches, type, and press Add. Chrome asks to let Fogar reach that one site. Feeds that already allow cross-origin reads work even if you say no.

optional permission · one site at a time

One list

Items from every source are merged newest first, and a link that shows up in two feeds is listed once. Show 10, 20, or 40. Each line says which source it came from.

click a headline · it opens on its own site

A dot on what is new

Anything dated after your last “Mark read” gets a dot. The widget refreshes when you open a new tab and the list is more than fifteen minutes old, or when you click Refresh.

Straight talk

Headlines, not a reading app.

The rest of Fogar is a new tab that answers from a model running in your browser. See what leaves in each mode.

  • It shows titles and a line or two of summary. Clicking opens the article on its own site. There is no built-in article view.
  • Read state is one moment in time: “Mark read” clears every dot. There is no per-item read, star, or archive.
  • No OPML import, no folders, no full-text search of past items. The list holds the newest 10, 20, or 40.
  • It refreshes only while you open new tabs. Nothing polls in the background.
  • Hacker News searches come through hnrss.org, a free third-party service. If it is down, that source shows an error and the rest still load.
Questions

Fair questions, short answers.

Do I need an account?

No. There is nothing to sign up for and no Fogar server. Your browser fetches each feed directly from its site and keeps the list on this device.

Which feed formats work?

RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0, Atom, and JSON Feed. If you only know the site, paste its address: Fogar looks for the feed the page advertises and remembers it.

Why does Chrome ask for permission when I add a source?

An extension can read another site only with permission for that site. Fogar asks for the one site you just added, at that moment, and never for all sites up front. Some feeds allow cross-origin reads and work even if you decline.

Can I follow Hacker News or a subreddit?

Yes. Pick “Search Hacker News” and type the words you care about, and new matching stories arrive through hnrss.org. For one subreddit, paste its feed address, such as reddit.com/r/sourdough/.rss, or use “Search Reddit” to watch all of Reddit for your words.

How does it know what is new?

It remembers when you last clicked “Mark read”. Every item dated after that gets a dot. The first time, everything is new.

Can I import my subscriptions from another reader?

Not yet. There is no OPML import, so sources are added one at a time. A site address is enough for most of them.
